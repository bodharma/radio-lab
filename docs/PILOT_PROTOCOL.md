# Radio Lab pilot protocol

Prepared 27 October 2026. **Protocol only: no instructor sign-off, participant recruitment, learner sessions or efficacy study has been completed.** No invitations or messages have been sent.

The first pilot asks whether people can use the lessons and transfer an idea to a new situation. It cannot establish that the app is the fastest preparation method, that it treats ADHD/autism, or that its scores predict an official examination result.

## 1. Complete a competent instructor review first

Invite a suitably qualified, currently licensed UK instructor to review a frozen app build, source-audit version, RSGB syllabus and EX307 edition. A licence alone does not establish teaching or safety expertise; ask an appropriate specialist about any unresolved electrical, RF or legal claim.

Use a review sheet with these columns: topic; syllabus item; claim/model state; cited source and section; correct/needs change/uncertain; proposed correction; reviewer/date; resolved in build. Check the following five groups:

1. Licence powers and definitions, current call-sign rules, supervision, overseas and ship/airborne distinctions, encryption exceptions and EMF scope.
2. Every equation, unit, example and graph at normal and boundary settings. Check that the drawn path and the explanation depict the same fault; distinguish a missing message from a missing RF stage.
3. Safety advice, model limitations and any instruction that a beginner could interpret as permission for mains, battery, antenna or work-at-height activity.
4. Question keys, plausible distractors, diagrams, syllabus tags, reference lookups and the distinction between course modules and official exam sections.
5. Imported material versus authored teaching, source rights/privacy, accessibility controls and all unresolved entries in [CONTENT_AUDIT.md](CONTENT_AUDIT.md).

Record actual findings and the reviewer’s scope. Do not label the product “instructor validated” merely because a reviewer saw a demo. Resolve incorrect keys, misleading mechanisms and unsafe instructions before learner sessions; retain uncertainty explicitly where it cannot yet be resolved.

## 2. Recruit a small voluntary feasibility sample

Begin with **3–5 consenting adult learners** who have varied radio experience and access preferences. This is a practical small pilot, not a statistically powered sample or an efficacy threshold. Invite input from autistic/ADHD learners if they wish to participate, but do not require diagnosis disclosure to select text, motion, pacing or navigation options.

Provide a short information sheet: purpose; what a session involves; optional data sharing; ability to pause/stop without penalty; who can see shared data; retention/deletion arrangement; contact for questions. Agree the arrangement before collecting anything. Use pseudonymous pilot IDs, not call signs or exam candidate numbers. No payment, recruitment, external account creation or data collection is authorised by this document alone.

## 3. Run one bounded learning session

Allow up to **60 minutes of active work**, with learner-controlled breaks. Keep the same target concept through the session when switching is disruptive. The example timings are scheduling choices, not biological attention limits.

| Phase | Active time | Procedure and record |
|---|---:|---|
| Orientation and baseline | 10 minutes | Explain controls without teaching the target answers. Use 3–5 unseen baseline items spanning prediction, explanation and a simple application. Record prior topic exposure and correct/incorrect/unsure; do not count confidence as knowledge. |
| Guided lesson | 20 minutes | Learner predicts, changes a relevant visual control, observes the result and explains it in text. Offer defined terms and worked examples. Record task completion, help requested/given, observed confusion and accessibility barriers. |
| New transfer case | 10 minutes | Remove lesson hints. Use changed values, an unlabelled diagram or a new everyday situation, not the same bank item with shuffled options. Preserve the first independent answer before giving feedback. |
| Learner feedback and next plan | 10 minutes | Ask what helped, what was confusing and which control or wording should change. Accept written responses or skipping. Agree a delayed check, then show relevant feedback. |
| Flexible allowance | 10 minutes | Use for orientation difficulties, a chosen pause or more explanation. Record actual active time and elapsed time separately. Do not force a second topic to fill the hour. |

For every scored task mark **independent**, **after a hint**, **after a worked example**, **skipped** or **technical/access failure**. Do not convert assisted success into independent success. Store the scoring rubric and expected mechanism before sessions so explanations are marked consistently. A short correct sentence or correct visual action can demonstrate understanding; verbosity is not a scoring criterion.

## 4. Check retention and an unseen official paper separately

Arrange a brief **delayed check 2–7 days later**, with its exact interval recorded. Use new equivalent cases and no lesson or previous key on screen. Ask about intervening study; record it rather than assuming there was none. Give feedback after preserving the independent response. Delayed performance is separate from immediate enjoyment or task completion.

When a learner chooses to rehearse the whole examination, use an **official RSGB paper they have not previously seen**, the permitted current EX307 reference and the published 26-question/60-minute format. Obtain the paper from [RSGB’s official mock-exam page](https://rsgb.org/main/clubs-training/training-resources/mock-exams/); do not copy it into the app or reuse its answers as lesson hints. Keep the first submitted score separate from repeats. If access arrangements or an untimed format are used, label them clearly; do not silently compare that result with a standard timed attempt. Confirm actual examination arrangements with RSGB.

The official unseen paper is an additional rehearsal benchmark. It still does not prove that this app caused improvement or guarantees passing a real examination.

## 5. Share minimally, analyse descriptively and decide the next revision

Local learning storage is the default. Any export must be **voluntary, previewable and explicitly triggered**. Default export fields: pseudonymous pilot ID; build/bank/audit versions; topic and item IDs; independent/assisted status; correctness; active minutes; delayed-check interval; and optional ordinal usability ratings. **Exclude free-text reflections and answers, names, email addresses, diagnoses, call signs, exact timestamps and device identifiers by default.** A separate informed choice is required for any extra field, quotation, screenshot or recording. Check the actual app export against this protocol before using it; the protocol does not certify that an export implementation already complies.

Review individual trajectories and concrete usability problems: baseline → independent transfer → delayed transfer; help required; task abandonment; navigation errors; misleading visuals; and accessibility preferences. Report actual counts and missing data. With 3–5 self-selected participants, do not claim statistical efficacy, diagnosis-specific effectiveness or superiority over another method. Familiarity, prior experience, external study and tutor assistance are alternative explanations.

Fix incorrect content and repeated usability blockers before expanding. Write a brief decision record: evidence; change; rationale; next check; unresolved limitation. A later comparative study would need a separate design, suitable recruitment and ethical/privacy review; do not imply this feasibility pilot supplies it.

## Primary reference points

- [RSGB syllabus v1.6b, March 2026](https://rsgb.services/public/exams/specifications/260305_Syllabus_2024_v1.6b.pdf): examinable knowledge, understanding and official paper structure.
- [RSGB EX307, January 2026](https://rsgb.services/public/exams/exam-forms/260122_EX307_Foundation_v1.pdf): permitted specimen reference material.
- [Ofcom licence conditions and schedules](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/spectrum/emf/emf-amateur-licence-terms-and-conditions.pdf?v=386197): operating authorisation; a learning or pilot score grants none.

Pilot sizes, timings, assistance labels and decision rules above are this project’s proposed procedure. They are not externally validated efficacy claims.
